My Journey in Wildlife Ecology and Remote Sensing

2026

I am a wildlife ecologist and remote sensing researcher currently based in Padua, Italy. My academic and professional journey has been driven by a singular focus: understanding and mitigating the impacts of climate change and human activity on keystone species through data driven models.

My foundation in natural sciences began with a Bachelor of Science in Agricultural Marketing and Co-operation from the University of Agricultural Sciences in Bengaluru, India. This early exposure to agricultural systems provided a practical perspective on the intersection of human livelihoods and environmental management. I later worked as an intern at the Indian Institute of Sciences (IISc) in Bangalore, where I spent night shifts in the field and laboratory collecting data on the acoustic communication of Katydids. This experience grounded me in the rigorous methods of behavioural ecology and field data collection.

My transition into applied conservation occurred when I joined the Wildlife Research and Conservation Society (WRCS) as a Program Officer. Here, I managed a community based Asian Elephant conservation project in the human dominated landscapes of Karnataka. I worked directly with over 500 farmers to implement crop protection strategies and collaborated with forest authorities on elephant relocation operations. We also utilized GIS tools to map and protect critical elephant corridors. A portion of this conflict mitigation work was featured in a National Geographic documentary.

Seeking to scale my analytical capabilities, I moved to Europe to pursue a Master's in Forest Sciences at the University of Padua, specializing in Forest and Land Management. During an Erasmus exchange at Wageningen University and Research in the Netherlands, I deepened my focus on wildlife management. My thesis explored the spatial distribution of Human Elephant Conflict in the Western Ghats, evaluating the effects of forest mosaics, water availability, and landscape fragmentation.

Currently, I am a PhD candidate at the University of Padua, focusing on Remote Sensing, Biodiversity Conservation, and Environmental Modelling. My ongoing research bridges ecology and technology. I am evaluating how climate change reshapes species habitats using rigorous statistical techniques and Earth Observation data. A major component of my work involves developing automated geostatistical pipelines, utilizing tools like H2O AutoML and multi sensor data fusion, to monitor biodiversity at a continental scale.

Through all these experiences, my goal remains consistent: bridging wildlife conservation with data science to produce actionable insights for landscape restoration and species protection.
